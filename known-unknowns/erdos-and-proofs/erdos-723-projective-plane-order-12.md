# MOONSHOT - Erdos #723: a projective plane of order 12 (fallback: 6 MOLS of order 12)

## The problem

Must every finite projective plane have prime-power order? Orders 6 and 14 are ruled out by Bruck-Ryser-Chowla, order 10 by Lam's computer search, and the conjecture is proved for n <= 11. Order 12 is the smallest open case. A plane of order 12 is equivalent to 11 MOLS of order 12. The best known count of MOLS of order 12 is N(12) >= 5 per the Handbook table (not live-verified by the auditor; the verifier must confirm against the current Colbourn-Dinitz tables).

## What counts as a win

MOONSHOT: an incidence structure of 157 points and 157 lines, each line of size 13, with N N^T = 12I + J (disproves the prime power conjecture). FALLBACK RECORD: k mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order 12 (equivalently a TD(k+2,12)) with k STRICTLY GREATER (direction: max) than the order-12 entry of the current Colbourn-Dinitz/Handbook MOLS table AT VERIFICATION TIME, which the verifier must open (5 as of the researcher's reading on 2026-10-03, i.e. 6 MOLS needed).

## Partial wins

- **p1:** A DRAT/LRAT-checked proof (CNF regenerated by the verifier) that no (12,8,1) difference matrix exists over one of Z_2 x Z_6, A_4 or D_6 (i.e. 6 MOLS cannot arise that way from that group), provided this is not already in the literature.

## How a win is checked

Plane: build the 157x157 incidence matrix N and check N N^T = 12I + J, all row and column sums 13, entries 0/1. MOLS: each square is Latin (rows and columns are permutations of 0..11), and for every pair of squares all 144 ordered symbol pairs are distinct.

## Traps

False wins others have fallen for. A claim that walks into one of these will not survive the skeptics.

- Using order-10 nonexistence techniques as if they applied to 12.
- Mis-recording the current MOLS(12) record; check the Handbook of Combinatorial Designs table.
