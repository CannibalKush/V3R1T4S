## What is being checked

g(8,4) is the largest family of 4-subsets of {1,...,8} with no three members having pairwise equal intersections (empty common intersection allowed). There is no live table for g(n,r), so novelty means: has the value 24, or an exactly equivalent reformulation, been published or tabulated?

Complement reformulation. Taking complements in [8] maps a 4-uniform 3-sunflower-free family to an (8-4) = 4-uniform family with no three members having pairwise equal unions. So n-r = 4 here. This is NOT the Mantel case (n-r = 2, triangle-free graphs) and NOT the K4^- Turán case (n-r = 3, three edges on four vertices). On 8 points a 3-sunflower of 4-sets must have a core of size 2 (three disjoint pairs plus the core cover all 8 points) or size 3 (three sets through a common triple), so the problem forbids two configurations at once. That pair of configurations is not a single standard Turán problem, and I found no table of it.

## Reasoning on general theorems

- n-r = 2 (Mantel) and n-r = 3 (ex(n,K4^-), source 1) give g(6,4) = 9 and g(7,4) = 15, not g(8,4).
- For (8,4) the complement family is again 4-uniform with no three pairwise equal unions. I found no theorem or table covering it. The exact Duke–Erdős and Chung–Frankl results need large n and forbid a single core size, so they do not apply at n = 8.
- An easy bound (each triple lies in at most 2 members, so 4|F| <= 2·C(8,3) = 112) gives only g(8,4) <= 28, not 24. No general theorem I know of yields 24.
